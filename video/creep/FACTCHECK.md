# Fact check: USE IT OR LOSE IT!

Every factual line, the source behind it and how it is hedged. The three tales and the blackout are fictional
dramatisations; the facts spoken over them are real and sourced below.

| "Once AI arrived, experienced doctors working without it found fewer precancerous growths." / "One real study: after AI arrived, doctors working without it found growths in 22% of patients, down from 28%." | Budzyń et al., *Endoscopist deskilling risk after exposure to artificial intelligence in colonoscopy*, The Lancet Gastroenterology & Hepatology (2025): a retrospective observational study in four Polish centres of the ACCEPT trial. The adenoma detection rate (the share of colonoscopies finding at least one adenoma, a precancerous growth) in standard, non-AI colonoscopies fell from 28.4% in the three months before AI tools were introduced to 22.4% in the three months after, when non-AI procedures were mixed with AI-assisted ones (1,443 non-AI procedures). Observational: confounders are not ruled out. | "It's happened before: calculators took our sums, GPS our routes." (on screen also: autocorrect and spelling) | The client's examples: these tools took over the task (described as offloading, not as proven harm). | "took our sums / routes" (the task, not a claim of measured loss) |

## The reader's night (fiction, checked for plausibility)
- The medicine: aspirin. The NHS advice for a suspected heart attack is to call 999 and, if not allergic, slowly chew and
  swallow one adult 300 mg aspirin while waiting for the ambulance. The bottle in the house is low-dose 75 mg tablets
  (the common UK daily dose), so the dose is 300 / 75 = 4 tablets: one division she has not done in her head for years,
  in the dark, in a panic. Her scribbled guesses (3? 5? 30?) are crossed out.
- The ambulance: 999 on the landline gets through ("CONNECTED"); the wait is 40 minutes. In England in 2025 the average
  Category 2 response (heart attacks, strokes) ran at about 27-30 minutes against an 18-minute standard (an average, so
  many waits were longer); 40 minutes on a stormy night is plausible rather than extreme.
- The hospital is ten minutes' drive away, but she has never driven there without the sat-nav; her phone is dead.
- Her sister's number: she gets "07" (every UK mobile starts that way) and no further.
- The outcome is shown: her father slumped in the armchair, his hand loosening on the aspirin bottle, the bottle falling
  and the tablets scattering, his hand slipping off the armrest beside the candle; then the candle goes out.
