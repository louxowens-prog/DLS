# Fact check: USE IT OR LOSE IT!

Every factual line, the source behind it and how it is hedged. The three tales and the blackout are fictional
dramatisations; the facts spoken over them are real and sourced below.

| Line (as spoken) | Source and what it says | Hedge |
|---|---|---|
| "After three months with an AI, experienced doctors working without it found fewer precancerous growths." / "The study is real. After AI arrived, doctors working without it found precancerous growths in 22% of patients, down from 28%." | Budzyń et al., *Endoscopist deskilling risk after exposure to artificial intelligence in colonoscopy*, The Lancet Gastroenterology & Hepatology (2025): a retrospective observational study in four Polish centres of the ACCEPT trial. The adenoma detection rate (the share of colonoscopies finding at least one adenoma, a precancerous growth) in standard, non-AI colonoscopies fell from 28.4% in the three months before AI tools were introduced to 22.4% in the three months after, when non-AI procedures were mixed with AI-assisted ones (1,443 non-AI procedures). Observational: confounders are not ruled out. On screen: "22% / DOWN FROM 28% / PATIENTS WITH A GROWTH FOUND, NO AI", stamped REAL STUDY. | "found fewer"; "in 22% of patients, down from 28%" (rounded; per colonoscopy); the fictional Dr Hale is never presented as a real person |
| "Write. Navigate. Remember. Decide. Hand them all over... and you may lose every one." | The client's point; skill decay with non-use is well established: Arthur et al., *Factors that influence skill decay and retention: a quantitative review* (Human Performance, 1998) - 53 studies: substantial loss with non-use, larger after longer gaps, and cognitive tasks decay faster than physical ones. | "may" |
| "It's happened before: calculators took our sums, GPS our routes." (on screen also: autocorrect and spelling) | The client's examples: these tools took over the task (described as offloading, not as proven harm). | "took our sums / routes" (the task, not a claim of measured loss) |
| "In one study, heavy GPS users had worse spatial memory on their own, and declined faster over three years." | Dahmani & Bohbot, *Habitual use of GPS negatively impacts spatial memory during self-guided navigation*, Scientific Reports 10, 6310 (2020): 50 drivers; more lifetime GPS use went with worse spatial memory when navigating without GPS; in 13 retested three years later, more GPS use went with a steeper decline in hippocampus-dependent spatial memory. | "In one study" |
| "London cabbies who memorise the city grow more grey matter in the brain's map. Use it, and it grows." | Maguire et al., PNAS (2000): licensed London taxi drivers had larger posterior hippocampi, correlated with years of driving; Woollett & Maguire, Current Biology (2011): trainees who qualified (learning "the Knowledge") showed grey-matter growth in the posterior hippocampus. | "more grey matter in the brain's map" (the posterior hippocampus grew; Maguire 2000 also found the anterior part smaller) |
| "Nine in ten UK students use AI." | HEPI / Kortext, *Student Generative AI Survey 2025*: 92% of 1,041 UK undergraduates use AI tools (up from 66% in 2024); 88% for assessments; 18% would put AI-generated text directly into their work. | "UK" |
| "'Help me understand' builds a mind. 'Write it for me' doesn't." | The client's point; consistent with the learning research below (Bastani et al.) and the generation effect (producing material yourself improves retention: Slamecka & Graf, 1978). | - |
| "In a small MIT study, 83% of people who wrote with a chatbot couldn't quote one sentence of their own essay." | Kosmyna et al. (MIT Media Lab), *Your Brain on ChatGPT: Accumulation of Cognitive Debt when Using an AI Assistant for Essay Writing Task* (2025 preprint, 54 participants): in session one, 83% of the LLM group could not correctly quote a sentence from the essay they had just written, against about 11% in the search-engine and brain-only groups. Not yet peer reviewed. The study's chatbot was ChatGPT; the script says "a chatbot" to keep brand names out. | "small" |
| "In a school study, AI lifted practice scores 48%. Take it away: 17% worse than students who never had it." / "And in that school, an AI tutor that guided, not answered, largely avoided the drop." | Bastani et al., *Generative AI without guardrails can harm learning: Evidence from high school mathematics*, PNAS 122 (2025): ~1,000 Turkish high-school students; with a standard GPT-4 interface practice grades rose 48% (127% with the tutor version); when access was removed, the standard-GPT group scored 17% lower than the control; the guarded "GPT Tutor" largely mitigated the harm. | "largely" |
| "Pilots too: leaning on autopilot, their thinking skills fade first." | Casner et al., *The retention of manual flying skills in the automated cockpit*, Human Factors 56(8) (2014): 16 airline pilots; hand-flying (scanning, control) was largely retained, but the cognitive skills of manual flight (tracking position without a map display, deciding the next steps, recognising failures) showed more frequent and significant problems. | "leaning on autopilot"; "thinking skills fade first" (cognitive skills declined more than hand-flying skills) |
| "And machines quit at the worst moment, when you need the skill you stopped using." | Bainbridge, *Ironies of Automation*, Automatica 19 (1983): automation leaves the operator to take over when it fails - typically in abnormal, hard conditions - with skills that have deteriorated through lack of use. | - |
| "The danger isn't using AI. It's outsourcing the thinking before you've learned it yourself, and never doing it again." | The client's thesis; see Bastani et al. (learning) and Arthur et al. (decay with non-use). | - |

## The reader's night (fiction, checked for plausibility)
- The dose: the label reads "Dose: 0.125 mg / Each tablet: 0.25 mg" - half a tablet, the kind of fraction people fumble under stress.
- The landline: 999 is tried first and the display reads ALL LINES BUSY (storm); then she starts her sister's number and cannot finish it.
- The outcome is implied, not shown: the laboured breathing stops, the candle goes out, a second of black.
