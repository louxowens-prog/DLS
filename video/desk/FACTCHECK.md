# Fact check: "Education" (A Light for Every Desk)

The student, her kitchen, her exam and her teacher are a **dramatization** (labelled so on the end card: "a
dramatization · the studies are real"). The tutoring exchange is the client's own example (cars, visually, five
problems, where my reasoning went wrong). The maths shown is correct. Every number is from a published source:

| Claim in the video | Source | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| "Thirty-eight of us, one teacher, forty-five minutes. About a minute each." | Arithmetic on the scene (45 min / 38 pupils = 1.2 min) | Illustrative class; the brief says 20-40 students per teacher. OECD averages are 21 (primary) and 23 (lower secondary). |
| "For most of history, the best teaching was one teacher, one student, for families who could pay." | The client's brief ("historically available mainly to wealthy people") | General history (private tutors for the wealthy). |
| "In one famous study, tutored students beat ninety-eight percent of a regular class." / banner "the average tutored pupil outscored 98% of the class · Bloom, 1984" | Bloom, "The 2 Sigma Problem", *Educational Researcher* 13(6), 1984: "the average tutored student was above 98% of the students in the control class" | |
| "Later studies found a smaller, but still large, effect." / banner "a smaller effect · still one of the largest in education · VanLehn, 2011" | VanLehn, *Educational Psychologist* 46(4), 2011: median effect of human tutoring d = 0.79 across studies | Spoken caveat on Bloom's 2 sigma. |
| The tutor explains derivatives with cars (odometer = distance; speedometer = its rate of change, the derivative); visually as the slope of the tangent; five practice problems; the error in d/dx (2x+1)^2 found (power rule right, the inside's derivative 2 missing: 4(2x+1)); exam question d/dx (3x+2)^2 = 6(3x+2) | Standard calculus | The problems' answers on screen are correct (s = t^2 at t = 3: 6; s = 5t + 2: 5; x^3: 3x^2; 4x^2 - x: 8x - 1; (2x+1)^2: 4(2x+1)). |
| "In a study of about a thousand high-school students, a plain chatbot raised their practice scores. Then, on the exam without it, they did seventeen percent worse. A tutor that gave hints, not answers, avoided that drop." / bars "+48% practice, -17% exam" | Bastani, Bastani, Sungu, Ge, Kabakcı & Mariman, "Generative AI without guardrails can harm learning: Evidence from high school mathematics", *PNAS* 122(26), 2025: nearly 1,000 students in Turkey; GPT Base improved practice grades by 48%, then exam grades fell 17% when access was removed; the GPT Tutor version (hints, safeguards) improved practice by 127% and largely mitigated the harm | "no drop on the exam" = the harm was largely mitigated (no significant drop). |
| "At Harvard, physics students with an AI tutor learned more than twice as much, in less time, as in an active-learning class." | Kestin, Miller, Klales, Milbourne & Ponti, "AI tutoring outperforms in-class active learning", *Scientific Reports* 15, 17458 (2025): RCT with 194 undergraduates; median learning gains more than double those in the active-learning lesson, in less time on task | The AI tutor was built on research-based pedagogy (not a plain chatbot), as the line says: "Built that way, it works." |
| "Teachers who use AI weekly say it saves them about six hours a week." | Gallup & Walton Family Foundation, 2025 survey of US K-12 teachers: weekly AI users save an average of 5.9 hours per week (about six weeks a school year) | Self-reported, as the line says ("say"). |
| "Forty percent of people can't get schooling in a language they understand." / banner "have no schooling in a language they speak or understand" | UNESCO Global Education Monitoring team, *Languages matter* (Feb 2025): 40% of the global population does not have access to education in a language they speak or understand | |
| "In Nigeria, six weeks of after-school AI tutoring brought gains like one and a half to two years of ordinary school." | De Simone et al., "From Chalkboards to Chatbots: Evaluating the Impact of Generative AI on Learning Outcomes in Nigeria", World Bank Policy Research Working Paper, 2025: 6-week pilot (June-July 2024, Benin City), +0.31 SD overall; equivalent to 1.5-2 years of "business-as-usual" schooling | English learning, first-year senior secondary students. |
