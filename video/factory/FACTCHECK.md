# Fact check: "Extending human intelligence" (the thinking-factory video)

| Claim in the video | Source | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Cold open: "Seventeen doctors. Three years. And still no answer for her little boy. Then, one night, she found a way to think bigger." | TODAY (Sept 2023), "A boy saw 17 doctors over 3 years for chronic pain. ChatGPT found the diagnosis"; also covered by NEJM AI Grand Rounds (Courtney Hofmann with Dr. Holly Gilmer) | Tagged "A TRUE STORY"; the mother and son are only shown as silhouettes. |
| Payoff: "His mother typed every symptom into an AI, and it suggested tethered cord syndrome. A neurosurgeon checked the scans, and agreed. Alex had surgery." | Same TODAY report: Courtney entered Alex's symptoms and MRI notes into ChatGPT, which suggested tethered cord syndrome; a neurosurgeon reviewed the MRI and agreed; Alex had surgery | "The AI didn't replace her doctors. It gave her the reach to find the right one" - it produced a hypothesis; the specialist confirmed and treated. |
| "A researcher here spends three hours digging through fifty reports, and has no time left to think about them." / "Three hours of searching become three hours of thinking." | The client's brief (the example of fifty reports and three hours) | Illustrative, as in the brief. |
| On screen: "office workers spend 1.8 hours a day just searching · McKinsey" | McKinsey Global Institute (2012), *The social economy*: employees spend 1.8 hours every day (9.3 hours a week) searching and gathering information | |
| Newsreel: "40% better work" (Harvard & BCG consultant study, 2023) | Dell'Acqua, McFowland, Mollick et al., "Navigating the Jagged Technological Frontier" (HBS working paper, 2023): 758 BCG consultants; on tasks inside the frontier, those with GPT-4 did 12.2% more tasks, 25.1% faster, with more than 40% higher quality | |
| Newsreel: "40% faster · 18% better" (writing study, Science, 2023) | Noy & Zhang, *Science* 381 (2023): professional writing tasks, time fell 40%, quality rose 18% | |
| Newsreel: "Beginners gain most: +34%" (customer-support study, QJE, 2025) | Brynjolfsson, Li & Raymond, "Generative AI at Work", *Quarterly Journal of Economics* 140(2), 2025: +14% issues resolved per hour on average, +34% for novice and low-skilled agents | "And beginners gain the most!" |
| Interviews: "It read my loan papers to me in plain English." / "It argues with my essay before my teacher does." | Illustrative uses (explain / translate jargon; critique an argument) | Tagged "RE-ENACTMENT". |
| "Trust it blindly, and you think less" | Lee et al. (Microsoft Research & Carnegie Mellon), CHI 2025: survey of 319 knowledge workers - higher confidence in GenAI was associated with less critical thinking; higher self-confidence with more | On the lesson card: "→ think less · Microsoft & CMU, 2025". |
| "Harvard's consultants did nineteen points worse on the wrong task." | Dell'Acqua et al. 2023: on a task outside the frontier, consultants using AI were 19 percentage points less likely to produce correct solutions | On the card: "19 POINTS WORSE · on the wrong task · Harvard & BCG, 2023". |
| "Used well, you think more." / the host's rule "My machines think WITH you. Never FOR you." | The client's brief ("This does not necessarily replace thinking. Used well, it can increase the amount and depth of thinking") | |
| The thirteen uses of an intellectual amplifier | The client's brief | Each is shown on a room's door plaque and acted out in that room: Untangler (explain, translate jargon); Press (summarize, find what matters, find inconsistencies); Arguing Mirror (critique an argument, simulate opposing views, compare competing ideas); Kitchen of What-If (brainstorm, generate hypotheses, explore consequences, organize thoughts, turn vague ideas into plans, find the missing questions). |
