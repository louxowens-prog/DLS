# Fact check: THE HOLLOW SCHOLARS

Every factual line, the source behind it and how it is worded. Clara, the Governess, the villa and the interview are
fiction (a dramatisation); the facts spoken over them are real and sourced below.

| Line (as spoken) | Source and what it says | Wording / hedge |
|---|---|---|
| "AI could be the finest tutor ever built. In one university trial, students with an AI tutor learned twice as much." | Kestin et al., *AI tutoring outperforms in-class active learning*, Scientific Reports (June 2025): a randomised crossover trial with 194 students in a Harvard physics course (autumn 2023). Students learned more than twice as much from a custom AI tutor at home as from an in-class active-learning session, in less time (median 49 minutes). The tutor was deliberately built to make students work: it guided step by step instead of handing over answers. | "could be"; "one university trial"; "twice as much" (the paper says more than twice). The lesson at the end ("if it makes you do the thinking") reflects how that tutor was designed. |
| "It can also do the work for you. Essays. Homework. Code. Reports." (on screen: essays, homework, code, reports, discussion posts) | The brief's list. | - |
| "At a UK university, researchers slipped AI answers into real online exams. 94% went undetected. On average, they outscored the real students." (on screen: the AI's column labelled +1/2 GRADE) | Scarfe et al., *A real-world test of artificial intelligence infiltration of a university examinations system: a "Turing Test" case study*, PLOS ONE (26 June 2024), University of Reading: AI-written answers (GPT-4) were submitted for 33 fake students to real at-home online exams in the School of Psychology and Clinical Language Sciences. 94% were not detected, and on average the AI submissions got higher grades than real students' - by about half a grade boundary - and in 83.4% of comparisons they beat a random sample of real submissions. | "real online exams" = real take-home (unsupervised, online) exams in the university's system; no claim about supervised exams. The columns on screen are drawn close in height and labelled +1/2 GRADE. |
| "Ghost writers aren't new. Now they're free and instant." | Contract cheating ("essay mills") long predates AI: England made providing it for profit a criminal offence in the Skills and Post-16 Education Act 2022 (in force April 2022). | "free and instant": a description of chatbots, not a statistic. |
| "94% of UK students now use AI to help with assessed work. About 1 in 8 paste its words straight in." (on screen: 94% USE AI FOR ASSESSED WORK / MOSTLY TO EXPLAIN THINGS; 12% PASTE AI TEXT STRAIGHT IN) | HEPI / Kortext, *Student Generative AI Survey 2026* (HEPI Report 199, March 2026; Savanta survey of 1,054 full-time UK undergraduates, December 2025): 94% use generative AI to support assessed work (95% use AI in some form); the most common uses are explaining concepts, summarising and structuring ideas; 12% directly include AI-generated text in assessed work, up from 3% in 2024. (The 2025 survey's figures were 88% and 18%.) | "now" = the latest survey; "to help with" (mostly legitimate help, and the sign says so); "about 1 in 8" = 12%. |
| "Proven AI cheating at UK universities tripled in a year. And that's only the ones who got caught." | The Guardian, Freedom of Information investigation (June 2025): almost 7,000 proven cases of cheating with AI tools at UK universities in 2023-24, 5.1 per 1,000 students, up from 1.6 per 1,000 in 2022-23 (more than threefold); figures to May suggested about 7.5 per 1,000 in 2024-25. Experts quoted called recorded cases the tip of the iceberg, and the Reading study above shows how much goes undetected. | "tripled" (x3.2, per 1,000 students); "only the ones who got caught". |
| "You can pass a programming course, and still not be able to program." | The brief's example; supported by the trial below (comprehension and debugging fall when AI writes the code). | Stated as a possibility ("can"). |
| "In a new trial (2026), coders learning a new tool with AI scored 50% on a test afterwards. Coding by hand: 67%. The biggest gap? Debugging." | Anthropic, *How AI assistance impacts the formation of coding skills* (29 Jan 2026): a randomised controlled trial with 52 mostly junior software engineers learning a new Python library (Trio). The AI-assisted group averaged 50% on a quiz about the concepts they had just used; the hand-coding group 67% ("17% lower ... nearly two letter grades"). The largest gap was on debugging questions. The AI group finished about two minutes faster, not statistically significant. Participants who used AI to ask for explanations kept more. | "coders learning a new tool" (junior engineers, not students); "a test afterwards" (a quiz minutes later). |
| "Do this for years, and degrees stop meaning what they say. Even the honest ones." | The brief's point (credentials become less reliable); follows from the undetected-submission and misconduct data above. A credential that cannot distinguish earned from generated work loses value for every holder. | Reasoned consequence, not a statistic. |
| "So universities are bringing back handwritten exams, and employers face-to-face interviews." | Handwritten exams: The Wall Street Journal (May 2025) reported blue-book exam booklet sales up 80% at UC Berkeley and about 30% at Texas A&M over two academic years and nearly 50% at the University of Florida, as professors return to in-class, handwritten exams because of AI. In-person interviews: Google's chief executive confirmed in 2025 that the company would reintroduce at least one in-person interview round amid concern about AI-assisted cheating in remote interviews; Cisco and McKinsey have reported similar moves, and Deloitte reintroduced face-to-face interviews for its UK graduate scheme. | No company named in the video (brands kept out). |
| Real-life scenario: an AI-free interview (phones left in a tray labelled PHONES; "I'll ask again. Why does this code crash?"), six rejections, "Fifty-three thousand pounds of debt." | Fiction, built to be plausible: technical interviews often ask candidates to read and debug code without tools, and employers are moving to in-person rounds (above). £53,010 is the average loan balance of English higher-education borrowers entering repayment in financial year 2024-25 (Student Loans Company / Department for Education, *Student Loans in England: Financial Year 2024-25*, provisional). | The debt figure is the national average, rounded. |
| "UNESCO calls for AI that is human-centred, age-appropriate and carefully governed." (on screen: age 13+ in classrooms, trained teachers, privacy rules, tools checked before use) | UNESCO, *Guidance for generative AI in education and research* (7 September 2023): a human-centred approach; governments should regulate generative AI (data protection and privacy, age limits), set a minimum age of 13 for using AI tools in the classroom, train teachers, and validate tools for ethical and pedagogical appropriateness. | The brief's three adjectives; the on-screen details are from the guidance. |
| "Yet in its 2025 survey, fewer than 1 in 5 universities had a formal AI policy." (on screen: UNIVERSITIES WITH A FORMAL AI POLICY, 2025: 19%) | UNESCO survey of 400 UNESCO Chairs and UNITWIN networks in 90 countries (run at Digital Learning Week, 2-5 September 2025): 19% of their institutions had a formal AI policy, a further 42% had guidance under development. (UNESCO's May 2023 survey of 450 schools and universities had found fewer than 10% with formal guidance.) | "in its 2025 survey": institutions in UNESCO's own networks, not a census of every university. |
| "Ask it to teach you, not to do it for you. If you can't explain it without the machine, you don't know it yet." | Practical advice drawn from the trials above: the guided tutor (Kestin et al.) and the coders who used AI to ask for explanations (Anthropic) kept their learning. | Advice, not a statistic. |

## Pronunciation notes
- "UNESCO" is spelled "Yoonesko" for the voice so it is said "yoo-NESS-koh".
- Numbers are spelled out for the voice and shown as figures in the captions.
